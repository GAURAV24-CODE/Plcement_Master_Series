DAY 03 — REMAINDERS → UNIT DIGIT → CYCLICITY → POWERS

REMAINDERS

Remainder = The value left after division.

DIVISION RULE

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder

Example:

17 ÷ 5

5 × 3 = 15
Remainder = 2

IMPORTANT

Remainder is always smaller than the divisor.

0 ≤ Remainder < Divisor

REMAINDER OF ADDITION

If:

A leaves remainder R₁
B leaves remainder R₂

Then:

(A + B) remainder
= (R₁ + R₂) remainder

EXAMPLE

17 ÷ 5 → Remainder 2
13 ÷ 5 → Remainder 3

17 + 13 = 30

30 ÷ 5 → Remainder = 0

UNIT DIGIT

Unit Digit = Last digit of a number.

Example:

347 → Unit digit = 7

IMPORTANT

For multiplication/powers,
focus only on the last digit.

EXAMPLE

23 × 47

Unit digits:
3 × 7 = 21

Unit digit = 1

CYCLICITY

Cyclicity = Repeating pattern of unit digits in powers.

IMPORTANT CYCLES

2 → 2, 4, 8, 6
3 → 3, 9, 7, 1
4 → 4, 6
7 → 7, 9, 3, 1
8 → 8, 4, 2, 6
9 → 9, 1

EXAMPLE

2⁵

Cycle of 2:
2, 4, 8, 6

5 ÷ 4 → Remainder 1

Answer = 2

POWERS

Power = Repeated multiplication.

Example:

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

LAST DIGIT OF POWER

Use the cyclicity pattern.

Example:

3⁷

Cycle:
3, 9, 7, 1

7 ÷ 4 → Remainder 3

Answer = 7

QUICK METHOD

1. For remainder → use division.
2. For unit digit → take last digit.
3. For large powers → find the cycle.
4. Divide exponent by cycle length.
5. Use the remainder to select the digit.

IMPORTANT

If exponent ÷ cycle length gives:

Remainder 0 → Use the last value of the cycle.

Otherwise → Use the value at the remainder position.
